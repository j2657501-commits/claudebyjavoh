Структура Task 1 (Academic)

  1. Introduction — перефразируй условие одним предложением: The line graph illustrates / compares / gives information about …
  2. Overview — 2 главные тенденции без цифр: Overall, it is clear that … while … Без overview выше 5.0 за Task Achievement не поставят.
  3. Body 1 и Body 2 — детали с цифрами, сгруппированные по смыслу (например, что росло и что падало). Сравнивай, а не перечисляй.

Никогда не пиши своё мнение и причины — только то, что видно на графике.

Полезная лексика для Task 1

  • Рост: rise, increase, grow, climb, go up; сильный — surge, soar, rocket; a sharp / steady / gradual / slight increase
  • Падение: fall, decline, decrease, drop, dip; plummet, plunge
  • Стабильность: remain stable / steady, level off, plateau at, stay at around
  • Пики: peak at, reach a high of, hit a low of, bottom out at
  • Сравнение: twice as high as, three times the figure for, considerably higher than, by contrast, whereas, while
  • Приблизительность: approximately, roughly, just under, just over, nearly, about a third, the vast majority
  • Процесс: first of all, in the first stage, once X has been done, after that, subsequently, finally; пассив — the clay is dug out, is then mixed with…
  • Карты: was replaced by, was converted into, was extended, was demolished, was relocated to; to the north of, adjacent to, in the south-west corner

Practice Task 1.1 — Line graph

The graph below shows the employment rates of men and women aged 18–34 in Australia between 1976 and 2011. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words.
Employment rate (%)19761981198619911996200120062011
Men8986847879808381
Women5256616366697273
Model answer (Band 8+), 178 words

The line graph compares the proportions of Australian men and women aged 18 to 34 who were in work over a 35-year period from 1976 to 2011.

Overall, although men were more likely to be employed throughout the period, the gap between the sexes narrowed considerably, as the female rate rose steadily while the male rate fell slightly.

In 1976, almost nine in ten young men (89%) had a job, compared with just over half of young women (52%). Male employment then declined gradually to 84% in 1986 before dropping more sharply to a low of 78% in 1991. After that it recovered slowly, reaching 83% in 2006, and ended the period at 81%, eight percentage points below its starting level.

By contrast, the figure for women increased in every five-year period. It climbed from 52% to 63% by 1991 and continued to rise to 72% in 2006, before levelling off at 73% in 2011. As a result, the difference between the two groups shrank from 37 percentage points at the beginning to only 8 by the end.

  1. Raw clay is dug out of the ground by an excavator and transported by lorry to the factory.

Task 1 General Training — письма (2026)

  1. Formal. You use your local library regularly. Write a letter to the librarian. Say what you like about the library, describe something you are not satisfied with, and suggest improvements.
  2. Formal. You want to rent a car for a holiday. Write to the rental agency. Explain what type of car you need, give the dates, and ask about the cost and conditions.
  3. Formal. You bought some clothes online but were not satisfied. Write to the company. Describe what you bought, explain the problem, and say why you need a replacement urgently.
  4. Formal. You are due to move into a rented apartment but need to delay. Write to the landlord. Explain why, say when you can move in, and ask what can be arranged.
  5. Semi-formal. Write to your manager asking for a salary increase. Describe your current role, explain why you deserve the increase, and say what you plan to do in future.
  6. Informal. You have recently moved to a new home. Write to an English-speaking friend. Explain why you moved, describe the new place, and invite them to visit.
  7. Informal. A friend has got engaged. Write to them. Say how you found out, how you feel about the news, and suggest meeting to celebrate.
  8. Informal. You are moving away. Write to your neighbours to say goodbye, thank them for their help, and say how to stay in touch.
  9. Informal. Invite a friend to a photography exhibition: say what it is, why they would enjoy it, and suggest arrangements.

Тон: formal — Dear Sir or Madam … Yours faithfully (имя неизвестно) или Dear Mr Brown … Yours sincerely; informal — Hi Anna … Best wishes / Take care. Каждому из трёх пунктов — свой абзац.

Фразы

  • Intro: It is often argued that… / There is an ongoing debate about whether… / In recent years, … has become increasingly common.
  • Позиция: I firmly believe that… / While I accept that…, I would argue that… / In my view, the benefits clearly outweigh the drawbacks.
  • Доводы: The main reason for this is that… / A further point is that… / This is largely because…
  • Пример: For instance, … / A clear illustration of this is… / This can be seen in…
  • Уступка: Admittedly, … / It is true that… However, … / Although some would claim that…, this ignores the fact that…
  • Вывод: In conclusion, although…, I am convinced that… / To sum up, …

Частые ошибки, которые стоят баллов

  • Ответ не на все части вопроса (например, обсудил только одну точку зрения) — потолок 5.0 за Task Response.
  • Меньше 250 слов; заученные «шаблонные» абзацы не по теме.
  • Позиция меняется от вступления к выводу.
  • Списки доводов без развития: лучше 2 идеи с объяснением и примером, чем 5 коротких.

Образец эссе (Band 8+)

Museums and historical sites are mainly visited by tourists rather than local people. Why is this the case? What can be done to attract more local people?
Model answer, 289 words

In many cities, the people queuing outside famous museums and monuments are far more likely to be foreign visitors than residents. This essay will examine why local people tend to stay away and suggest how they might be encouraged to return.

There are two main reasons for this pattern. The first is simply familiarity. People who have grown up near a castle or gallery often feel they can go at any time, so the visit is postponed indefinitely, whereas a tourist with only three days in the city treats it as a priority. The second reason is that many such places are designed with outsiders in mind. Ticket prices are set at a level tourists will accept, permanent displays rarely change, and opening hours coincide with the working day. A resident who visited once on a school trip therefore has little incentive, and little opportunity, to go again.

Fortunately, both problems can be addressed. To begin with, institutions could offer residents free or heavily discounted entry, as several European cities already do, together with late opening on at least one evening a week so that working people can attend. In addition, museums need to give locals a reason to come back by staging temporary exhibitions, talks and family workshops that change regularly. If these events draw on the history of the surrounding community, for example by displaying residents' own photographs and stories, people are more likely to feel that the museum belongs to them rather than to visitors.

In conclusion, local people neglect their own heritage mainly because it is always available and seldom aimed at them. Lower prices, more convenient hours and a changing programme rooted in the community would go a long way towards bringing them back.
